What is Cosine Similarity in Embeddings?
Cosine similarity is a mathematical method used to measure how similar two vectors are by comparing the angle between them.

In your Indian Kitchen Asma RAG project, it helps your system find recipe transcripts that are most relevant to the user's question.

Let's understand it step by step.
1. First, what is an embedding?
An embedding is a list of numbers that represents the meaning of text.

For example, imagine your embedding model converts these sentences into vectors:
Text	Example embedding
“How to make biryani?”	[1, 2]
“Biryani cooking steps”	[2, 4]
“How to repair a laptop?”	[-2, 1]
These are simplified, made-up vectors for learning. Real embedding models usually generate vectors containing hundreds or thousands of numbers.

Texts with similar meanings often have embeddings pointing in similar directions.
2. What does cosine similarity do?
Imagine your system receives this question:

“How do I make biryani?”

Your database contains two transcript chunks:
- Chunk A: “Steps for preparing biryani with rice and spices.”
- Chunk B: “How to repair a laptop keyboard.”

The embedding model converts the question and both chunks into vectors. Cosine similarity compares their directions to estimate which chunk is more semantically similar to the question.
